Savings Interest Calculator

This high yield savings account calculator turns your bank’s APY into a balance. $5,000 plus $200 a month at 4.5% APY grows to $19,629.26 in 5 years, including $2,629.26 of interest. For comparison, the FDIC national average savings rate was 0.38% as of August 17, 2026.

How much interest will my savings earn?

Enter the APY your bank quotes, your opening deposit, your monthly deposit and the number of years. The calculator converts the APY to a monthly rate, (1 + APY)^(1/12) − 1, then compounds your balance monthly, with each deposit added at the end of its month. The result shows your balance, the interest earned and the total you deposited.

Here is the default example at two rates, both for 5 years:

4.5% (sample high-yield rate)$19,629.26$2,629.26

In both cases you deposit $17,000 ($5,000 plus 60 × $200). The FDIC figure is a weighted average of the rates insured banks and credit unions pay, based on the $2,500 product tier.

Savings rates can change, so a 5-year projection assumes today’s rate holds the whole time. The 4.5% rate is a sample, not an offer from any bank. Enter the rate on your own account.

Interest is generally taxable, and the calculator shows it before tax. If you plan to invest rather than save, the investment calculator uses the same kind of projection with a return you assume.

What is the difference between APY and APR?

APY, or annual percentage yield, is the yearly return on a deposit including the effect of compounding. Its calculation is set by federal rules, so it compares fairly across banks. APR describes a yearly rate on borrowing. For loans, the CFPB explains that APR includes the interest rate plus points and certain fees. For comparing savings accounts, APY is the number to use.

Under Regulation DD, Appendix A, the APY measures the total interest paid based on the interest rate and how often it compounds. For accounts with no maturity date, such as savings accounts, the calculation assumes a 365-day term.

Because compounding is already built in, an APY should not be compounded again. That is why this calculator converts the APY to an equivalent monthly rate instead of dividing it by 12. For example, a stated 4.5% rate compounded monthly works out to an APY of about 4.59%. Compounded daily, it is about 4.60%.

If an account quotes only an interest rate, ask for the APY so you can compare offers fairly. For general compound growth at a nominal rate, use the compound interest calculator.

Savings account vs CD

A savings account keeps your money available, and its rate can change. A certificate of deposit (CD) commits your money for a set term, so the account’s disclosure matters: check whether the rate is fixed and what happens if you withdraw early. Which suits you depends mostly on whether you may need the cash before the term ends.

The FDIC national averages as of August 17, 2026 show how the products compare on average:

Individual banks may pay well above or below these averages. The money market and CD figures average the $10,000 and $100,000 product tiers.

To project a CD, enter its APY, set the monthly deposit to $0 and set the years to the CD’s term. The result assumes interest stays in the account and compounds. If your CD pays interest out to another account instead, the balance shown will be higher than you actually hold.

Frequently asked questions

How much will $10,000 make in a high-yield savings account?

At 4% APY, $10,000 earns about $400 in one year if the rate holds and you add nothing. At the FDIC national average of 0.38% (August 17, 2026), it would earn about $38. Rates on savings accounts can change, and interest is generally taxable.

How much will $100,000 make in a high-yield savings account?

At 4% APY, $100,000 earns about $4,000 in a year, or roughly $333 a month on average. At 0.38%, it earns about $380. Check the APY your bank pays today and remember the rate may change. The interest shown is before tax.

How much do I need in a high-yield savings account to make $1,000 a month?

$1,000 a month is $12,000 a year. At 4% APY you would need about $300,000, and at 4.5% about $266,667. Those figures assume the rate stays the same and are before tax. If the bank lowers its rate, the income falls.
