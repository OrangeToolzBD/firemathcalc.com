Net Worth Calculator

How do I calculate my net worth?

Net worth is total assets minus total liabilities. List everything you own at its current value, list every debt at its current balance, and subtract. The Federal Reserve uses the same definition in its Survey of Consumer Finances: the difference between a family’s assets and its liabilities. If your debts are larger than your assets, the result is negative, and the goal becomes moving it toward zero.

What counts as an asset?

An asset is anything you own that has a money value you could realistically get. The main groups are cash, investments, retirement accounts, property and valuable personal items. Retirement accounts count even though you may not be able to use the money yet without tax or penalties.

  • Cash: checking, savings, money market accounts and CDs.
  • Investments: brokerage accounts, bonds and other holdings, at current market value.
  • Retirement accounts: 401(k), 403(b), IRAs and similar accounts.
  • Property: your home, other real estate and vehicles.
  • Business interests and other items with a clear resale value.

The Federal Reserve treats retirement accounts as financial assets in its survey. In 2022, 54.3% of families held them, and the median value among those families was $86,900.

For accounts with a tax bill waiting, such as pre-tax retirement savings, some people also track a lower “after-tax” figure. That is optional, but label it clearly if you do.

Leave out everyday belongings like furniture and clothing unless you have a specific reason to include them. They are hard to value and seldom sell for much.

Should I include my home and car?

Yes, include both, and include the debts on them too. The Federal Reserve’s survey counts vehicles and real estate as nonfinancial assets, and mortgages on the home as debts. Your home often has the largest effect: in 2022 the median net worth of homeowners was $396,200, compared with $10,400 for renters.

Value them carefully:

  • Home: use a realistic sale price, not an optimistic estimate. Some people subtract likely selling costs.
  • Car: use today’s resale value, and update it each time you calculate rather than keeping the price you paid.

Many people also track a second figure that leaves out their home, because you cannot spend home equity without selling or borrowing against the house. For FIRE and retirement planning, that investable figure is usually the one that funds withdrawals. The retirement calculator uses savings, not home value.

Tracking both numbers gives a fuller picture: total net worth for your overall financial position, and investable net worth for retirement readiness.

Frequently asked questions

How do you calculate net worth?

Add the current value of everything you own, including cash, investments, retirement accounts, your home and vehicles. Then subtract everything you owe, such as a mortgage, car loans, student loans and credit cards. The result is your net worth. The calculator above does the sums as you type.

What’s a good net worth by age?

There is no single right number, but the Federal Reserve’s 2022 survey gives medians by age of the family head: $39,000 under 35, $135,600 for 35–44, $247,200 for 45–54, $364,500 for 55–64, $409,900 for 65–74 and $335,600 for 75 or older. Your own goals matter more than the comparison.

Is a 401(k) considered part of net worth?

Yes. A 401(k) is an asset you own, and the Federal Reserve counts retirement accounts, including 401(k)s and IRAs, as financial assets in its net worth figures. Some people also note that pre-tax balances will be taxed when withdrawn, so they track an after-tax version as well.

What is the average net worth in the U.S.?

In the Federal Reserve’s 2022 Survey of Consumer Finances, mean family net worth was $1,063,700 and the median was $192,900, in 2022 dollars. A mean far above the median reflects very large balances at the top of the distribution, so the median is the better guide to a typical family.
